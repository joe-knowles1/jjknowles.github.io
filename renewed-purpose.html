Renewed purpose

20/11/2026

I’m feeling a lot of pride and determination around service design at the moment. This has culminated in a kind of renewed purpose.

I’ve been reading Bad Services by Lou Downe, and feel fortunate we have someone who is able to articulate what a lot of people are experiencing.

The NHS needs service design, whether it knows it or not. It needs people who think about the gaps between products more than ever. AI is not going to make end-to-end services better — at least not at first. If anything, it’s going to make them worse.

This is mainly because AI is currently being used to solve specific problems (e.g. ambient voice summaries for assessments), but not interoperability and the handoffs between systems.

We also need to think about the fact that the NHS is both a service and a product organisation. This means we have teams built around services and teams built around products.

A product centred organisation would optimise and reduce duplication within products — they would work for multiple services — but the services themselves might suffer end-to-end for their specific circumstances.

A service centred organisation would optimise individual services, perhaps to the detriment of reuse.

As a profession we need to find the right balance. We work within services (hopefully), and we have to build better services, yes, but we also need to elevate our work so that we can reduce inefficiency and duplication across services.

To make services work, but to do it with our heads up, so we’re not all individually solving problems separately.

We need community more than ever.

My feeling of purpose comes from the fact that, having seen some of this stuff first hand in the last couple of weeks, we are needed. I’ve seen organisations trying to deliver services, doing them differently from other organisations, and in some cases doing them differently within organisations.

And while I’ve been thinking about my purpose, I’ve also been thinking about the purpose of the services I’m working on. It seems so obvious, but conversations tend to be around signals — such as waiting lists — rather than the presumed purpose — to make people better.

I feel convinced that service design, and UCD in general, has a huge opportunity to add value to this organisation — whatever the organisation ends up looking like.

And I feel that if there are service designers in the NHS reading this who are struggling with their work, try to keep heart. Move to a different area. And keep on.

I think we can do this.

P.S. if you are struggling, I would highly recommend reading Bad Services, it will almost certainly help.
