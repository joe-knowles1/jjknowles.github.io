Top level community thoughts - part two

13/11/2026

Last week I mentioned I was going out to do more research this week with a similar provider to last week, albeit one which provides different services in the community.

Again it was fascinating. I feel like I ought to give serious consideration to rebranding/ training to become a user researcher — I spent the first seven years of my career doing an awful lot of research and I clearly miss it.

Two things stood out to me.

The first is that we saw a lot of duplication. In many ways this is unsurprising, but I suppose the part that sticks out is that the systems are doing such a bad job that the humans — who care a great deal about their patients and doing the job well — don’t want to make mistakes.

In this case that articulated itself as them manually compiling an excel report, pulling out all numbers (such as referrals, deferrals (put off appointments done mainly due to capacity), breaches (most serious cases that have missed the timelines they are supposed to be seen in — very few in this case), staff numbers etc.

Then, all of that was duplicated onto a white board in the office. At 9am they all sat and went through it all, to discuss cases, priorities, sickness, and ensure the numbers they had were correct in both the spreadsheet and on the board.

This took about eight people about 45 minutes.

While some of that discussion is important and couldn’t be replaced (‘how are we going to deal with these numbers?’), surely a lot of it could be with one reliable, easy-to-use source of the truth (‘are these numbers correct?’)?.

The second thing I took from yesterday seemed smaller but was shocking to me.

For one service they would create a plan for a patient to follow. Instead of emailing it to the patient — because they were worried the patient would then have their email address — they printed it and sent it out. I actually wrote ‘wow’ in my notes at that one.

No shade on the people doing this, but there are solutions that would enable a better experience for the patient (and a more timely one), reduced printing costs, and probably better outcomes. All for want of a ‘do not reply’ email address.

But how could they know? They’re not IT experts, they’re practitioners. There seems to be too many clinical staff dealing with admin, feeling stretched and unable to stop and think about some of the things they’re doing. It’s not their fault.

They set up a clinic to get people in, see more in a day and thus tackle the waiting list. The room was booked, but no one was actually invited to come because they were too busy and it just didn’t happen.

These are the people we need to help. And we’re going to bloody do it too.
